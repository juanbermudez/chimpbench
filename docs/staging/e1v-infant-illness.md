# E1v: the infant illness deaths of the 0.5 arm (6 October 2026)

The open question of `e1v-prereg.md` §8.4: "Whether the energy ledger changes an infant's illness risk was not checked
here." Two of the four 12-month runs at `pithFibreSwallowed` 0.5 scored a first-year mortality (T-DEM-1, band 0.11–0.19)
far above the band. Is that (a) chance in a small-count row, (b) an effect of the wadging input through the energy
ledger, or (c) an effect of something else that differs between those runs?

No simulation was run. Everything here is read from the code (branch `infant-illness`, from track-e e0cf866) and from the
saved 12-month runs: the per-seed parts and the end checkpoints of the twelve runs (S39, W50, W25; rngSalt 0–3; 5 seeds
each). Every number is printed by `scripts/e1v-infant-illness.ts` (the two generated blocks below); the confidence
figures in the answer are judgements and say so.

## 1. Answer

**(a): chance, in a row one 12-month run cannot resolve. Not (b). Not (c), but for one scored death.**

Confidence (judgement): that these deaths did not come through the energy ledger, about 95%; that the surplus at 0.5 is
chance and not some cause I could not see in saved data, about 85%.

1. **No death in question went through the energy ledger.** The code has one path from energy state to the illness
   hazard (reserves → condition → health → a hazard multiplier; §2), and it leaves a mark: health below
   `hazardHealthThreshold` at death, or the label "starvation". Every first-year illness death of the twelve runs was
   drawn at multiplier 1.00: full health, no injury, mother alive (K2, K3; table G5). Each was the plain slow-step draw
   `random(world) < p` at the baseline infant hazard (K4), the same number in all three arms.
2. **The energy state points the other way.** Infants and their mothers at 0.5 are in the same state as at 0.25 and in
   a better one than in S39 (K5; G7). The arm with the worst energy state (S39) and the arm with the same state as 0.5
   (0.25) both have ordinary infant mortality, so there is no order along the input (T6). The path of §2 was exercised
   (some infants fell below `condLow`, mostly in S39: K5) and no first-year death came of it.
3. **Nothing else that differs between the runs explains it.** The illness deaths of the two high runs are spread over
   seeds, communities and months, with no respiratory signs in the community near any of them and no mother dead (K2;
   G5). There is no first-year orphan or starvation death in any run (K3). `rngSalt` only re-seeds the random stream
   after the world is built (`src/sim/generation.ts:309-312`), the same salts are not high in the other two arms (G4),
   and the same mothers' infants survive in most of the other realizations (G6). The one exception: in M12-W50 one of
   the scored deaths is not a first-year death in simulation truth but an outbreak death of a founder the observer
   aged under one (K8); that is one scored death of the row, by the observer's design
   (`src/field/observer.ts:148-149`: a founder's age is an estimate; table G3b).
4. **The test.** The 0.5 arm is higher than the other two, but not beyond the spread of the runs by any test, with the
   run or the infant as the unit (K6; T2, T3, T4b); the closest is the upper tail of the permutation of the scored row.
   The row cannot do better: Poisson noise alone gives one 12-month run a
   standard deviation wider than the whole band, and one death moves it by most of the band's width (K4).
5. **What is left unexplained: the level, not the arm.** Against the code's own baseline hazard the twelve runs
   together have more first-year illness deaths than expected, and the surplus is the 0.5 arm's, mostly one run's (K7;
   T1, T4). Deaths at one year or more sit on their expectation with the same generator and the same loop (K7; T1b).
   Because each death was a draw at the baseline probability (point 1), the surplus can only be the draws themselves:
   a tail event of the size K7 gives, in a row that was picked out after the fact because it was the one that moved;
   or a property of the random stream at this one age class that saved data cannot show. I rate the first far more
   likely, and it is the part §4 can settle.

## 2. The code path

**How an infant comes to die of "illness".** The slow step (`src/sim/tick.ts:98-103`, every `slowEveryTicks` ticks:
`src/sim/state.ts:13-14`) calls `slowLife` and then `slowDisease`. In `slowLife` (`src/sim/life.ts:220-268`), for every
living animal: condition is read (`:243`), health is moved toward its target (`:245-246`), and one number is drawn
(`:261-262`):

    const p = 1 - Math.exp(-hazard(c, P, 1 / Math.max(1, world.ageRate)) * bioDays / 365.25);
    if (starved || c.health <= 0.02 || random(world) < p) {

`hazard()` (`:199-210`) is a constant per age class (`:202`, `hazardInfant` under one year), less the expected epidemic
hazard that `disease.ts` delivers separately (`:207`), times a multiplier (`:208`):

    h *= 1 + Math.max(0, P.hazardHealthThreshold - c.health) * P.hazardHealthWeight + c.injury * P.hazardInjuryWeight;

The label is chosen after the fact (`causeFor`, `:212-218`, called at `:265`): "orphaned infant…" if unweaned with the
mother dead, "complications of wounds" above an injury of 0.5, "starvation" if the reserve is exhausted or condition is
below `condLow`, "old age" above 45, otherwise **"illness"**. So "illness" is the residual label of the baseline draw:
mother alive (or weaned), not badly wounded, condition at or above `condLow`.

**"respiratory illness (outbreak)"** is a different draw (`src/sim/disease.ts:74-75`): when a case ends, the odds of
death are the base fatality, an odds ratio under five years, one at thirty or more, and the outbreak's virulence. No
term reads condition, health or reserves. Arrival is a Poisson draw per community (`:17-35`); spread is by party
co-membership (`:45-57`).

**Every place the energy ledger can reach the illness hazard.**

1. Reserves → condition: `ledgerSlow` (`src/sim/energy.ts:826-830`), condition = `ledgerCondSet` × (1 + reserves ÷ store).
2. Condition → health: `life.ts:245`, the target loses (condLow − condition) ÷ condLow once condition is below
   `condLow`; health follows it (`:246`, a time constant of half a day).
3. Health → hazard: `life.ts:208` above, once health is below `hazardHealthThreshold`; `:262` kills outright at 0.02.
4. Upstream of 1 for an infant: milk, own food and growth are entries of its own ledger, so they act through its
   reserves; a newborn starts in its mother's pregnancy condition (`src/sim/reproduction.ts:167`,
   `src/sim/energy.ts:258-263`). The mother's state reaches the infant through milk, through that starting value and
   through her death (the orphan label, `life.ts:213`), and through nothing else.
5. A death drawn while condition is below `condLow` is labelled starvation (`life.ts:265`), so this path yields the
   label "illness" only in the hours after condition has climbed back over `condLow` while health has not yet
   followed, and the dead animal's health then reads below 1.
6. A respiratory illness lowers the health target by `epidemicHealthDrop` (`life.ts:245`), which leaves it above the
   threshold: by itself it does not multiply the baseline hazard.
7. Other readers of health: strength (`src/sim/hierarchy.ts:40`; a mother's defence against infanticide,
   `src/sim/conflict.ts:310`), conception (`src/sim/reproduction.ts:121`) and the observation packet
   (`src/sim/observe.ts:132`). The growth record follows condition (`life.ts:248`) and feeds strength only. None feeds
   the illness draw.
8. The input itself: `pithFibreSwallowed` is read in one function (`src/sim/energy.ts:115-120`, used at `:130`); it
   changes the dry matter and fibre of the fallback food as swallowed. It can reach mortality through 1–3, or through
   the random stream: all randomness is one generator (`src/sim/rng.ts:4-9`), so any change of behaviour re-deals every
   later death draw. That second route is chance by construction; it is what `rngSalt` does on purpose.

So a path from energy state to illness hazard exists, it is narrow, and it leaves a signature (health below 1 at
death, or the starvation label). The data in §3 show the signature on none of these deaths.

**Code versions (table G0).** The two arms ran at one commit; `life.ts`, `disease.ts`, `rng.ts`, `reproduction.ts`,
`hierarchy.ts` and `energy.ts` are identical there and on this branch (`git diff --stat` between the two is empty for
them). The S39 reference ran on an earlier source tree (its commit id is from before the history was re-authored; the
commit of today's history with that `src` tree is 3729416, found with `git rev-parse <commit>:src`): `disease.ts`,
`rng.ts`, `reproduction.ts` and `hierarchy.ts` are identical to the arms', and `life.ts` differs by one call of stage
E1t that is outside `hazard()` and `slowLife`. Every 12-month run continues its 6-month run from that run's last
checkpoint (G0, "resumed from"), so the 6-month runs hold no draws of their own and are not tabulated.

## 4. What would settle it, and what to do

- **Nothing to fix in `src/` for wadging.** The input does not reach the illness draw in these runs; the open question
  of §8.4 closes as "no, by code and by record".
- **The level (answer, point 5) needs new draws, and they are already being made.** The three-year runs in progress
  (bench-y3: two at 0.5, two at 0.25, one reference; only their `run.json` status was read here) run past the 12 months read here. Prediction, registered before they are read: first-year illness deaths per infant-year at the baseline
  hazard of table G1 in every arm; T7 gives the count that would reject it and the power. If their runs keep end
  checkpoints, `scripts/e1v-infant-illness.ts all --group …` reads them as it read these; their first 12 months at
  salts 0 and 1 repeat runs counted here (same seed, same parameters) and must be left out (the script has no window-start option yet). If the
  count at 0.5 rejects the baseline again and 0.25 does not, the question reopens as a question about the stream, not
  about energy.
- **Without waiting:** more `rngSalt` values at 0.5 and at 1 for 12 months; T7 gives the run-years needed.
- **The judge.** T-DEM-1 should not enter a per-run fitted sum at 12 months: one run's Poisson noise is wider than the
  band (K4). Read it pooled over the salts with its Poisson interval (table G3), as the rare rows are read.
- **A readout that would have answered this at once** (optional): sum the slow-step draw's probability by age class in
  the viability readout, so every run prints expected beside observed deaths, and keep condition beside health on the
  dead record (health survives `slimDead`; condition is dropped with the hidden state after 30 days).
