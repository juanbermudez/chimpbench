# E1k pre-registration: nursing mothers' remaining deficit

Registered 2 October 2026, before any run of changed code. Track E, stage E1k, branch `e1k-deficit` (from `track-e`
6f2ee87). Any new switch is 0 by default in both profiles and is read only with `energyLedger` 1, so the compressed
goldens and the field pin in `tests/sim-track-e.test.ts` cannot move.

**Rule served.** Field values of behaviour are targets, never inputs. No weight or input is tuned to hit a reserve
trend, a feeding time or a grooming share; a miss is a finding. The viability line (0.05% of the store a day) is a
design guard and is not changed here.

## 0. The problem (E1i 5-seed confirm, e1i-prereg.md §8.1)

Reference **B2** = R + `ledgerFoodEnergyFix` + `ledgerSatiationReserve` + `ledgerLactGut` (R: handoff §3). On 5 seeds ×
60 days: no starvation death; nursing mothers eat 277 min and 788 g of dry matter a day (T-ENE-2, T-ENE-3 in band) but
absorb 0.959 of what they spend, and their reserves fall 0.149% of the store a day; juveniles 5–12 y fall 0.071%/day.
Mothers' foregut is ≥ 95% full in under 10% of daylight and their daylight hunger is 0.49. Mothers of infants ≥ 2 y
groom 33.7% of daylight (other females 12.1%), after 1.49 interruptions per daylight hour, 77% from their own infant
("began grooming me"). T-ACT-3 (grooming share, band 0.08–0.18) is out for females.

## 1. Step 0: is the 0.05%/day line realistic for nursing mothers and juveniles? (audit, sources only)

### 1.1 What the line means in energy

The usable store at the set point is `ledgerReserveKcalPerKg` 1,300 kcal/kg × body mass (assumed, range ×2). For a
31.3 kg mother that is 40,690 kcal, so the line (0.05%/day) is 20 kcal/day, **1.2% of her daily spending** (1,707
kcal/day in B2). B2's −0.149%/day is 61 kcal/day (3.6% of spending; −71 kcal/day absorbed − spent). For juveniles 5–12
y (about 28 kg on average) the line is about 18 kcal/day, 1.4% of their spending (1,241 kcal/day); B2's −0.071%/day is
about 26 kcal/day. No field method resolves a balance of 1–4% of daily expenditure directly: the field reads energy
balance as an index (urinary C-peptide), ketone presence, or body mass on a scale.

**Reading.** The field gives the *sign* of mothers' energy balance by lactation stage, not a size: depressed (read as
negative) in months 0–6 postpartum, then a net gain through the second year, and a sustained gain before cycling
resumes. No study reports a rate of mass or fat change in kcal, kg or % per day for wild nursing mothers or juveniles,
and ketones (the marker of substantial fat mobilisation) were never systematically detected at two sites. Weaned
juveniles gain lean mass with age (samuni2020; emeryThompson2016); no source reports a sustained loss of reserves in
juveniles.

### 1.4 Verdict of the audit

- **The line is not stricter than wild mothers for the infant ages the model's window holds.** The field reports no
  sustained negative balance of measurable size after 6 months postpartum; its direction from 6 months on is a net gain.
  B2's −0.149%/day is the wrong sign for every infant age present, not a field-sized loss that a strict line rejects.
  The integrator's suggestion (e1i-prereg.md §8.1, "the line may be stricter than wild mothers") is not supported for
  mothers of infants ≥ 6 months (moderate confidence: abstract-level evidence, C-peptide is an index, n = 17).
- **Juveniles:** no field rate; the field direction is growth in lean mass, with no reported reserve loss. The line stays
  a design guard; no band.
- **Staged (never applied):** `docs/staging/e1k-targets.patch.json` proposes a direction band on the staged T-ENE-5 row
  read on the ledger: mothers whose youngest infant is 0.5–2 y, mean daily reserve change ≥ −0.05% of the store (zero
  within the design guard; sign from emeryThompson2012). B2 sits below it (−0.083 and −0.186%/day). Months 0–6: a
  negative balance is expected, size unsourced, no band. Infants ≥ 2 y: the abstract's "sustained period of energy
  gain" before cycling resumes points the same way, but its timing is not in the abstract: no band.
- What a size would need: emeryThompson2012's monthly C-peptide values (full text, not reached) and a calibration of
  C-peptide against energy balance in kcal (none known for chimpanzees), or repeated weights by reproductive state
  (pusey2005, full text not reached).

### 1.5 Where in the window the deficit sits (found during the audit, before any run of this stage)

The integrator's energy JSONs keep each class's daily mean reserves (`traj`), so the 5-seed window splits in halves
(`season_table.py`, stage scratch; least squares over each half):

| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| female, lactating | −0.027 / −0.143 | −0.084 / −0.296 | −0.851 / −1.035 | −0.043 / −0.298 |
| juvenile 5–12 y | −0.002 / −0.035 | +0.006 / −0.046 | −0.122 / −0.199 | −0.028 / −0.140 |
| female, other | −0.004 / −0.018 | −0.006 / −0.022 | −0.036 / −0.141 | −0.021 / −0.092 |
| adult male | −0.002 / −0.013 | −0.001 / −0.014 | +0.005 / −0.059 | −0.007 / −0.058 |

In every arm every class loses faster in the window's second half (run days 60–90, 27 Nov–27 Dec). B2's mothers are
at −0.043%/day in the first half (inside the line; the quick window is this half) and −0.298%/day in the second.
The food supply, computed without simulating behaviour (`season_probe.ts`: the phenology crop of every tree is a pure
function of time), falls in the same half: the summed crop drops by 35% (seed 48), 52% (7), 23% (11), 47% (21) and
56% (5) from run day 60 to 90 (it changed by 0% to −23% from day 30 to 60), and the young-leaf season factor of the
forage field falls from 1.11 to 0.89.

**Consequence for the audit.** The field reports lower energy balance when fruit is scarce (emeryThompson2009, vale2020:
C-peptide rises with fruit; staged T-ENE-4), so a fall of reserves through a 23–56% fruit decline is the field's
direction, not by itself a viability failure. A line of 0.05%/day for every class over a window that includes this
decline tests the season as much as the mothers. What remains a model question is the *size* of the mothers' fall
relative to the other classes (×3–5 in the second half) and whether mothers raise their feeding time when food
falls, as the field's chimpanzees do (vale2020: they fed for less time when food was abundant).

## 4. Step 1: diagnosis (readouts defined before the run; smoke-tested on 2 days, seed 48, B2)

Tool: `scripts/intake-diagnose.ts` (E1i's, extended here; simulation truth, read-only). Run: B2, seeds 48 and 7,
30-day burn-in + 60 days, `--split 30` (every class again for window days 0–29 [early] and 30–59 [late]: the quick
window and the seasonal decline). Daylight = `environment.daylight > 0.1`. E1i's D1–D6 are unchanged; new:
- **Classes:** lactating females also by the youngest infant's age in years (0.5–1, 1–2, 2–3, 3–4, ≥ 4 y); juveniles
  5–12 y (energy-diagnose's class). Decisions of animals under `rgMinAge` (8 y; argmax rules, which the tap does not
  see) are read after each tick from `decisionVersion` and the candidate list.
- **G1 mother–infant grooming:** an episode is a run of daylight ticks in which the mother grooms her own unweaned
  infant or it grooms her, in contact (`phase` 1 of the groom act). Reported per mother class: episodes per daylight
  hour, length, share of daylight, mother-only / infant-only / both, who started (who groomed in the first tick), who
  ended (who groomed in the last tick) and why (the ender's decision in the ending tick: gate reason → new act), the
  mother's hunger and foregut fill at the start and end, and her own act while she only receives.
- **G2 the infant's initiative:** unweaned infants' daylight decisions by age: share choosing to groom the mother,
  that option's score against the infant's best other option, the infant's hunger and social need (1 − social).
- **G3 the mother's acceptance:** her decisions when the interrupt is her own unweaned infant's "began grooming me":
  what she chose, P(groom back) and P(feeding) from the menu's softmax, the groom-back score against the best feeding
  score, her hunger and fill.
- **D7 decisions with appetite and a foregut that is not full:** every daylight decision (RG draws and argmax) with
  hunger > 0.4 and foregut fill < 0.95: what won, P(feeding) at draws, the mean margin of each winner over the best
  feeding option.
- Energy per half from the reference's `energy-diagnose` `daily` series: eating minutes, dry matter, absorbed ÷ spent.

"Name the term that holds the last 4%": the readout that accounts for the mothers' missing intake (≈ 70 kcal/day,
about 12 eating minutes at their absorbed rate) in the half where it occurs.

**Reading: the terms that hold the last 4%.**
1. *Where:* the 5-seed deficit sits in the window's second half (§1.5). In the quick window mothers are at −0.036%/day
   and absorb 0.993 of what they spend; in the decline they absorb 0.938. No class answers the decline with more
   feeding time (eating minutes 278 → 272 mothers, 261 → 252 juveniles, 239 → 234 other females, 247 → 249 males), against
   the field's direction (staged T-ENE-9: feeding time higher in low-fruit months). Juveniles' deficit is this term
   alone (−0.000 early, −0.123 late; their main competitor at decisions with appetite and room is play, 20.7%).
2. *Who among mothers:* the loss rises with infant age (−0.06%/day at 0.5–1 y, −0.18 to −0.20 at 2–4 y). Mothers of
   infants ≥ 2 y eat 34.8% of daylight against 38.4% for mothers of younger infants (about 27 min/day, ≈ 165 kcal at
   their absorbed 6.1 kcal/min) at a higher hunger (0.53 against 0.42), and rest 4.8% against 16.6%.
3. *The term:* **the mother–infant grooming loop, scored by constant partner terms while both animals' social need is
   met.** 93% of the episodes start with the infant (33.6% infant, 59.2% same tick: the infant begins and the mother
   answers at once); the infant chooses it from a score of 0.74 against 0.38 for its best other option at a social
   need of 0.02; the mother answers 86% of the time (P(groom back) 0.863, P(feeding) 0.064) with the groom-back option
   at 1.02 against 0.33 for her best feeding option at hunger 0.51, and at a social need of 0.04–0.06; during an
   episode (41 min) her hunger rises from 0.36 to 0.58 and her foregut empties from 0.82 to 0.67. At decisions with
   appetite and room, grooming her infant is the first non-feeding winner (28.9%, margin +0.25 over the best feeding
   option). Adults groom others at a social need of 0.35–0.81, so outside this dyad grooming has a need behind it.
   The binding terms in the score are the bond (0.4 × bond), kin (0.2), reciprocity (0.2) and invitation (0.3 + 0.4 ×
   need) weights, which do not depend on the groomer's own state (candidates.ts, groom offer).
4. Not the gut: the foregut is ≥ 95% full in 9% of daylight in both halves; not nursing (E1i §5).

## 6. Iteration 1 (registered 2 October 2026, before its run): grooming in the mother–infant dyad valued by the groomer's own social need (`groomNeedDyad`)

**Physiology.** The motivation to be groomed is a regulated need that grooming feeds back on: in monkeys "Opiate
receptor blockade increases the motivation to be groomed, while morphine administration decreases it" (keverne1989,
[M]). More generally a stimulus's value depends on the internal state it serves (alliesthesia, cabanac1971, [H] in
humans): an incentive is worth what the matching drive makes it worth. The model already has the state (`social`,
filled by grooming given and received, play and nursing, emptied at 0.035/h awake); the dyad's grooming ignores it.

**Mechanism** (`src/sim/candidates.ts`, groom offer; 0 = today, hash-identical). Between a mother and her own unweaned
offspring, in either direction, the option's social terms are multiplied by the groomer's need n = 1 − social:
score = persistence + n × (0.55 + 0.4 × bond + 0.2 × kin + 0.2 × reciprocity + rank + 0.7 × invited − female non-kin
offset) − costs (tension, distance, 0.6 × hunger, rain, night, the under-5 term). At n = 1 it is today's score exactly;
at n = 0 only persistence and costs remain. No new number (the weights are today's; the product form is the design
assumption). Outside the dyad nothing changes (scope: the term the diagnosis implicates; the general form is the open
problem). Not chosen: weighing the mother's acceptance against her hunger (the diagnosis shows the ignored state is her
social need, and mothers keep social time with offspring on poor diets, sabbi2024).

**Arm G1** = B2 + `{"groomNeedDyad":1}`. Tools (each once, from a frozen checkout of the commit that registers this):
`e-bench --quick --workers 1`, `energy-diagnose --seeds 48,7 --burn-in 30 --days 60`, `intake-diagnose --seeds 48,7
--burn-in 30 --days 60 --split 30`. Reference: B2 and its three re-draws (§3; the intake readouts from four runs too).
Smoke test of the readouts with the switch on (2 days) before the arm.

**Predictions (by hand, before any run of the switch).** Arithmetic: at the mother's need of about 0.05 the groom-back
score is about 0.05 × 2.0 − 0.3 (hunger 0.5) ≈ −0.2, below feeding (0.33) and rest; her need then refills only by
0.035/h, so she grooms her infant again only after hours (n ≈ 0.3 gives ≈ 0.6 − 0.3 = 0.3); the infant's own start at
n ≈ 0.02 falls below the offer floor. Mothers of older infants should then look like mothers of younger ones.

## 7. Known defects and caveats in the code under test

- `scripts/intake-diagnose.ts` (G3, interrupt attribution): the interrupter is found by the reason text's name prefix
  (`r.startsWith(o.name)`), as in E1i; a name that is a prefix of another could be misattributed (none checked).
- `scripts/intake-diagnose.ts` (decisions under rgMinAge): read after the tick; "interrupt" is inferred from
  `lastIntrAt` within the tick, so a decision at an act's end in the same tick as an interrupt counts as an interrupt.
- `scripts/energy-diagnose.ts` (traj): seed-mean daily reserves; class composition changes with births and age
  classes (as in E1i).
- No defect found in the code under test that bears on the measurement.
