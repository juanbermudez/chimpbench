# E1k pre-registration: nursing mothers' remaining deficit

Registered 2 October 2026, before any run of changed code. Track E, stage E1k, branch `e1k-deficit` (from `track-e`
6f2ee87). Any new switch is 0 by default in both profiles and is read only with `energyLedger` 1, so the compressed
goldens and the field pin in `tests/sim-track-e.test.ts` cannot move.

**Rule served.** Field values of behaviour are targets, never inputs. No weight or input is tuned to hit a reserve
trend, a feeding time or a grooming share; a miss is a finding. The viability line (0.05% of the store a day) is a
design guard and is not changed here.

## 0. The problem (E1i 5-seed confirm, e1i-prereg.md §8.1)

Reference **B2** = R + `ledgerFoodEnergyFix` + `ledgerSatiationReserve` + `ledgerLactGut` (R: handoff §3). On 5 seeds ×
60 days: no starvation death; nursing mothers eat 277 min and 788 g of dry matter a day (T-ENE-2, T-ENE-3 in band) but
absorb 0.959 of what they spend, and their reserves fall 0.149% of the store a day; juveniles 5–12 y fall 0.071%/day.
Mothers' foregut is ≥ 95% full in under 10% of daylight and their daylight hunger is 0.49. Mothers of infants ≥ 2 y
groom 33.7% of daylight (other females 12.1%), after 1.49 interruptions per daylight hour, 77% from their own infant
("began grooming me"). T-ACT-3 (grooming share, band 0.08–0.18) is out for females.

## 1. Step 0: is the 0.05%/day line realistic for nursing mothers and juveniles? (audit, sources only)

### 1.1 What the line means in energy

The usable store at the set point is `ledgerReserveKcalPerKg` 1,300 kcal/kg × body mass (assumed, range ×2). For a
31.3 kg mother that is 40,690 kcal, so the line (0.05%/day) is 20 kcal/day, **1.2% of her daily spending** (1,707
kcal/day in B2). B2's −0.149%/day is 61 kcal/day (3.6% of spending; −71 kcal/day absorbed − spent). For juveniles 5–12
y (about 28 kg on average) the line is about 18 kcal/day, 1.4% of their spending (1,241 kcal/day); B2's −0.071%/day is
about 26 kcal/day. No field method resolves a balance of 1–4% of daily expenditure directly: the field reads energy
balance as an index (urinary C-peptide), ketone presence, or body mass on a scale.

**Reading.** The field gives the *sign* of mothers' energy balance by lactation stage, not a size: depressed (read as
negative) in months 0–6 postpartum, then a net gain through the second year, and a sustained gain before cycling
resumes. No study reports a rate of mass or fat change in kcal, kg or % per day for wild nursing mothers or juveniles,
and ketones (the marker of substantial fat mobilisation) were never systematically detected at two sites. Weaned
juveniles gain lean mass with age (samuni2020; emeryThompson2016); no source reports a sustained loss of reserves in
juveniles.

The window holds no mother of an infant under 6 months (the one phase in which the field reports a depressed balance),
and the model's mothers lose reserves at every infant age present, **most with infants of 1–4 y**, the ages at which

their only energetic readout.

### 1.4 Verdict of the audit

- **The line is not stricter than wild mothers for the infant ages the model's window holds.** The field reports no
  sustained negative balance of measurable size after 6 months postpartum; its direction from 6 months on is a net gain.
  B2's −0.149%/day is the wrong sign for every infant age present, not a field-sized loss that a strict line rejects.
  The integrator's suggestion (e1i-prereg.md §8.1, "the line may be stricter than wild mothers") is not supported for
  mothers of infants ≥ 6 months (moderate confidence: abstract-level evidence, C-peptide is an index, n = 17).
- **Juveniles:** no field rate; the field direction is growth in lean mass, with no reported reserve loss. The line stays
  a design guard; no band.
- **Staged (never applied):** `docs/staging/e1k-targets.patch.json` proposes a direction band on the staged T-ENE-5 row
  read on the ledger: mothers whose youngest infant is 0.5–2 y, mean daily reserve change ≥ −0.05% of the store (zero
  within the design guard; sign from emeryThompson2012). B2 sits below it (−0.083 and −0.186%/day). Months 0–6: a
  negative balance is expected, size unsourced, no band. Infants ≥ 2 y: the abstract's "sustained period of energy
  gain" before cycling resumes points the same way, but its timing is not in the abstract: no band.
- What a size would need: emeryThompson2012's monthly C-peptide values (full text, not reached) and a calibration of
  C-peptide against energy balance in kcal (none known for chimpanzees), or repeated weights by reproductive state
  (pusey2005, full text not reached).
