How a chimp decides, and what is still prescribed.

ChimpBench is rebuilding its chimpanzees’ decisions so behaviour comes from the body, the senses, memory and company instead of clock hours, timers, dice rolls and tuned bonuses. This guide maps every kind of decision and marks each rule: still prescribed, replaced, measured, or a design choice.

Hover, focus or tap any box to see what it does. For a replaced rule you get the old rule and the mechanism that replaced it.

What “now” means

In the shipped model every Track E switch is 0, so today’s behaviour is still fully prescribed: 135 prescriptions.

This guide shows the candidate stack S9, the best integrated candidate so far: 39 switches run together in one integrated confirm (5 seeds, viable, safe at night). S9 is S3 plus E4g’s fix for following carers, E5a’s company valued by social need in place of six weights fitted to party size, the mother’s weaning decision by her own deficit in place of the weaning roll (E1n, E1o), two corrections of design stand-ins (growth that yields to the store, E1p; a return to a crown valued by the crop left, E3b), and E5d’s relationship upkeep: the social need rises by what bonds lose and only grooming meets it, in place of the two social timers, with grooming weighted by the groomer’s need and a company margin for every move toward a companion (E5b, E5d).

Stages not yet integrated, held off, recorded or still in progress are drawn as separate layers, dashed, and never counted in S9.

How to read this page

Each diagram follows one kind of decision from the body state that drives it to the act. Read the middle column top to bottom; boxes at the sides feed the step they point at. Every box carries one status, shown by its colour, its icon and its label.

  • Prescribed

    Still prescribed in S9. A clock hour, a timer, a dice roll, a quota, a tuned bonus or a field value of the behaviour itself states when or how often something happens. Named by its parameter or its line of code.

  • Replaced

    Prescribed on today’s model, replaced in S9. Its detail shows Before (the old rule, its parameters and values), Now (the mechanism), the switch that did it, the stage and its verdict.

  • Input

    Physiology or physics measured apart from the behaviour, with its evidence tag: H high, M medium, L low, or assumed.

  • Design

    A design weight or structure that states no outcome. Not counted as a prescription.

  • Not in S9

    A separate layer, dashed: a candidate not yet integrated, a defect fix, or a stage held off, recorded or in progress. A dashed pink arrow points at what it would change.

Hover, focus or tap

Point at a box, tab to it, or tap it for its detail. Click or tap pins the detail open; tap elsewhere or press Esc to close it. Every diagram also has a text version below it.

Expand

Opens a diagram full screen. Zoom with the scroll wheel, a pinch, or + and −; pan by dragging or with the arrow keys; 0 fits it to the screen. Esc, the close button or a click outside closes it.

What counts as a prescription

The prescription ledger’s rule: entries of the parameter registry classed outcome-encoding and in use under the field profile, plus lines of src/sim that compare the hour of the day, roll dice against a fixed number, or filter acts by the time of day. Score weights written into the code are design and are not counted.

Where the numbers come from

Every list, value, count and switch on this page is generated from scripts/prescription-ledger.ts; the words come from the stage pre-registrations and the code. See how this page is made.

01

The choice itself

The same machinery picks every act. Three of its parts still set how sharp and how sticky choices are by fixed numbers.

4 still prescribed0 replaced in S9of 4 on today's model

  • The rules policy is the same for every domain below; a model can replace the draw, never the checks (simulation.md §8).
  • E3 is not part of S9. Its stage stopped after three iterations (e3-prereg.md).

02

Feeding and foraging

Hunger now comes from an energy ledger and a gut. What is still fitted is how far a meal is worth walking, how fast a crown empties and how fast an animal walks.

6 still prescribed5 replaced in S9of 11 on today's model

  • On S9 the feeding, grooming and rest rows of the activity budget are in their bands (0.368, 0.101, 0.380), and the fruit share sits at its band's edge (0.783 against 0.6–0.78). One animal died on S9 in 5 seeds × 60 days, not of starvation (e-stack2-confirm.md).
  • Open: nursing mothers stop eating with room in the gut, so the limit is their appetite and their day, not the gut wall (e1h-prereg.md §9, e1i-prereg.md).

03

Drinking

Thirst timers are gone. Thirst is read from a water balance in millilitres, so animals drink when they run short, not on a schedule.

0 still prescribed6 replaced in S9of 6 on today's model

  • Drinking fell from 2.24 to 0.84 bouts per adult-day and walks to water from 22% to 10% of movement (e2g-prereg.md).
  • Adding the water ledger to the integrated stack (S2 → S3) cut males' daily path from 3.79 to 2.93 km and six prescriptions (e-stack2-confirm.md).

04

Sleep and the nest

The nest clock, the morning bout and the night menu are gone. Sleep pressure, a circadian sleep gate, light and company now decide when a chimp nests and when it leaves.

0 still prescribed18 replaced in S9of 18 on today's model

  • Nesting at dusk and an active day of 11 h 22 min emerge with no clock (e2a-prereg.md).
  • Still wrong by its one-site row: on S9 the observer scores 74% of departures before sunrise, against 18% for five Taï mothers in fruit-scarce periods. E2h found that row scored differently from the field, and that on S3 the real miss was nursing mothers leaving about an hour early, hungry and thirsty (e2h-prereg.md, e-stack2-confirm.md).

05

Rest and heat

The midday rest clock is gone. Rest and shelter follow a heat balance, which at Kibale's temperatures stores almost no heat.

0 still prescribed4 replaced in S9of 4 on today's model

  • Midday rest does not emerge: the heat balance stores almost no heat at Kibale's temperatures, so midday rest fell from 54% to 22%, and hot, mild and rainy days do not differ (e2a-prereg.md). It is not a digestive pause in the model either (e1b-prereg.md).

06

Nursing and weaning

Mothers pay for the milk their infants drink and decide, by their own deficit, when to let them suckle: the weaning roll is gone. Milk now falls with the infant's age and growth yields to the store, but weaning itself does not emerge; its age is still drawn.

0 still prescribed3 replaced in S9of 3 on today's model

  • On S9 nursing mothers lose 0.058% of their store a day (S8 0.060) and their balance sits between −21 and −44 kcal a day by infant age. Infants of 1–2, 2–3 and 3–4 years drink 271, 258 and 254 kcal a day (cap 307), more than on S6 because better-fed mothers refuse less (e-stack2-confirm.md, S9).
  • Infants' reserves fall 0.069% and 0.050% of the store a day at 0.5–2 and 2–5 years, and they grow 3.42–3.46 kg a year at 1–4 years, still about twice Gombe's 1.6 (e-stack2-confirm.md, e1p-prereg.md §3).
  • Open: nothing drives milk to zero before the drawn weaning age (e1o-prereg.md §5).

07

Grooming and play

Since S9 the social need is relationship upkeep: it rises by what the animal's bonds lose each day, only grooming meets it, and grooming's partner terms count only as far as the groomer needs grooming. Rough play is still a dice roll.

1 still prescribed2 replaced in S9of 3 on today's model

  • On S9 the grooming row is inside its band for both sexes (0.101; males 0.120, females 0.085; band 0.08–0.18; S8 0.197), and rest falls (0.380; S8 0.427) (e-stack2-confirm.md).
  • Open: the bond dynamics the need now follows are design (founder bonds, baselines of 0.2 and 0.6, 1.5% a day relaxation), and E5a values company by a need that company does not relieve (e5d-prereg.md §10).

08

Party following and joining

The six weights fitted to party size are gone: a companion's company is now worth what the animal's own social need makes it (E5a), with E4g's fix for following carers and a margin: every move toward a companion counts only the company it adds (E5b, E5d). Still fitted: how far a call is worth walking, and when a leaver tries again.

3 still prescribed6 replaced in S9of 9 on today's model

  • On S9 parties average 4.04 animals (band 3–9), males walk 2.11 km a day (S8 1.95), and the travel share is inside its band (0.182; males 0.21, females 0.16; band 0.12–0.25) (e-stack2-confirm.md).
  • E5a passed on five seeds on the reference stack (prescriptions 103 → 97, sums inside noise). On the integrated stack without E5b's margin it added half a kilometre of walking a day (e5a-prereg.md, e5b-prereg.md §8).
  • Open: party size does not track crop size, the party-size band (3–9) has no recorded derivation, and company is valued by a need that company does not relieve; only grooming does (e5a-prereg.md §7, e5d-prereg.md §10).

09

Calls

Six call rules that were hazards, coins, quotas or clock windows are now one value comparison, and since S5 what a listener does with a call is valued by company too. Greetings, the sound of calls and how far a call is worth walking are still prescribed.

6 still prescribed11 replaced in S9of 17 on today's model

  • On 5 seeds adult males call 0.59 times an hour (field 0.5–1.5) and arrival calls sit in the field's range; 11 prescriptions fewer (e4c-prereg.md §10).
  • Where calls meet company: on S4 males walked 3.43 km a day against 2.93 on S3, mostly approaches to callers valued in full even by animals already in company; with E5b's margin, 2.73 on S5 (e5b-prereg.md §8, e-stack2-confirm.md).
  • Cost carried forward: calls have no daily course (morning ÷ afternoon 1.02 against about 4.3 in the field) (e4c-prereg.md).

10

Aggression and displays

Hormone-like states now open escalated attacks, redirected aggression and rain displays. How a fight turns out is still dice.

8 still prescribed6 replaced in S9of 14 on today's model

  • Slow states keep the acts rare and well-timed: redirects come within a minute of the defeat and stay at 2–4% of decided conflicts (e4a-prereg.md).
  • Open: slow states cannot carry acute reactions, hence the fast state; the calling–testosterone link still fails because calls have no daily course (e4b-prereg.md, e4d-prereg.md).

11

Hunting

Hunting is still prescribed from the gap between hunts to how a hunt ends. A candidate that values a hunt as food is held off.

  • The observer meets colobus 2.7 times as often per follow-hour as at Kanyawara, mostly a scoring difference (e4f-prereg.md).

What is still prescribed

On S9, 74 prescriptions remain. These are the biggest, by how much of a chimp’s life they reach.

  • The choice itself (4)

    How sharp a draw is, how long an intention lasts, and the bonus for carrying on touch every decision. Urgency (E3), which would replace them, stopped after three iterations.

  • Rough play (1)

    A play bout with a much younger partner turns rough by a dice roll. The social need itself is no longer a timer: since S9 it rises by what relationships lose, and only grooming meets it (E5d).

  • Walking and foraging (6)

    Walking speed, three trip weights and the fruit eaten per hour, fitted to travel share and day range; plus a wild-cost multiplier that is 1 and probably counted by mistake.

  • Patrols (12) and killing (12)

    All of patrolling: a start window, a fitted hazard, joining scores, route odds and a release display. Gang attacks and infanticide are impulses and kill probabilities.

  • Fights (8) and hunts (5)

    How contests end, allies joining and charge quotas; the gap between hunts, the success curve, extra kills and meat eaten per hour. A hunt valued as food (E4e) is held off.

  • Life, death and care (13), mating and dispersal (4)

    Epidemics, snares, adoption, carrying the dead, bereavement and guardianship; a mating quota, a consortship hour, a transfer hazard and a disperser share.

  • Calls (6) and staying together (3)

    How calls sound, how often a greeting repeats and how far a call is worth walking; when a leaver tries again after a failed departure.

By the ledger’s kinds, the 74 are: 24 probability, 14 field-copy, 13 fitted, 8 quota, 4 clock, 4 hazard, 3 designed-from-target, 2 bonus, 1 dice (code), 1 timer.

Open problems

What the candidate stack still gets wrong, from the integrated confirms and the stage records.

  • Company valued by a need only grooming relieves

    E5a values a companion’s company by the social need, but company does not relieve it; only grooming does, so time that grooming no longer fills goes to seeking company rather than rest. On S9 every class walks 0.1–0.2 km a day more, and nursing mothers eat 17 minutes a day more. The bond dynamics the need follows are design (e5d, e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • The held-out score

    Held-out sums stay level with S8 on S9 (z −0.1, and +1.2 without the rare rows). The largest miss is still T-FOOD-10: the observer scores 74% of departures before sunrise, against 18% at Taï. That row rests on five Taï mothers in fruit-scarce periods and is scored differently; a staged scorer fix awaits the user (e-stack2-confirm.md, e2h).

  • Hunting above its band again

    35.5 hunts per community-year on S9, against a band of 5–25 (S8 28.0). The band was never scaled to the model’s 3–7 males (a scaled 4–11 is staged) and the observer meets colobus 2.7 times as often as at Kanyawara; three scorer fixes are staged, not applied (e4e, e4f).

  • Reserves a little lower

    On S9 juveniles lose 0.060% of their store a day (S8 0.046) and other females 0.045% (S8 0.031). Infants lose 0.069% and 0.050% at 0.5–2 and 2–5 years while they grow 3.42–3.46 kg a year, about twice Gombe’s 1.6 (e-stack2-confirm.md, e1p).

  • A row on few events

    T-IGE-3, the approach to strangers depending on own males, rests on few events: two S8 re-draws scored it at 30.5 and 22.3, the others under 1, so the sums are read without it (e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • Weaning does not emerge

    Milk falls with the infant’s age on S9 (254 kcal a day at 3–4 years) but nothing drives it to zero. Weaning age is still drawn, between 4.1 and 5.2 years; the ledger classes it as an input, although it builds the weaning-age target in (e1o, e1n).

  • Feeding together

    Party members feed one or two at a time (1.3 per crown) and a visit eats about 11% of a crown, so the crop never limits them, party size does not follow it (S9: 4.04 animals, band 3–9), and a return finds what the animal left. What makes them arrive and feed together is not in the model (e5c, e3b).

  • Patterns that do not emerge yet

    A midday rest (e2a) and a daily course of calls (e4c, e4d).

  • The gut’s size

    The only chimpanzee gut volume is one captive animal of unknown mass: 57–76 mL per kg at captive female masses, against the 83 assumed (handoff §4.1).

  • Everything is provisional

    Runs are capped at 90 days, and no switch goes on by default before the C11 calibration and the combined proof are frozen (handoff §1, §5.7).

How this page is made

The lists and numbers are generated; the words are written from the stage records and the code.

Statuses. scripts/decision-guide.ts runs the prescription ledger (scripts/prescription-ledger.ts, rules in scripts/lib/prescriptions.ts) on the field profile with every switch off, on S9, and on S9 with each layer. A box is still prescribed if its entries are in use on S9, and replaced if they were in use on today’s model and are not on S9. The switch named for a replaced entry is the one S9 switch that takes it out on its own (with the switches it needs); where several do, the one that needs least. --check fails when a box mixes statuses, when a counted entry is in no domain or no box, or when the page’s embedded lists differ from a fresh ledger run. Which stack the page shows is one constant in that script (STACK).
