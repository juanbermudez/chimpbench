How a chimp decides, and what is still prescribed.

ChimpBench is rebuilding its chimpanzees’ decisions so behaviour comes from the body, the senses, memory and company instead of clock hours, timers, dice rolls and tuned bonuses. This guide maps every kind of decision and marks each rule: still prescribed, replaced, measured, or a design choice.

Hover, focus or tap any box to see what it does. For a replaced rule you get the old rule and the mechanism that replaced it.

What “now” means

In the shipped model every Track E switch is 0, so today’s behaviour is still fully prescribed: 147 prescriptions.

This guide shows the candidate stack S39, the last integrated candidate of Track E: 56 switches run together in one integrated confirm (5 seeds, 60 days, viable and safe at night then). Over 12 months it is not viable: with all the pith’s fibre swallowed, animals starve (six deaths in 20 seed-runs; none in the 6-month runs), and with half or a quarter swallowed they do not (none in 20 each; e1v-prereg.md §8.4). S39 is S3 plus E4g’s fix for following carers, E5a’s company valued by social need in place of six weights fitted to party size, the mother’s weaning decision by her own deficit (E1n, E1o), growth that yields to the store (E1p), a return to a crown valued by the crop left (E3b), E5d’s relationship upkeep in place of the two social timers, hunts valued as food (E4e), feeding valued by its net energy rate (E3c), contests settled by assessment (E4h), the social clocks and quotas replaced by state (greetings, consortships, charges and the walk to callers; E5e), patrols decided by the males in place of the patrol clock, hazard and dice (E4i) and weighed only when a party truly joins up (E4j), hunts that succeed or fail by the pursuit itself, in place of a success curve and a kill die (E4k), no fitted choice temperature: the best option is taken as the animal values it, with crops out of sight drawn from its belief (E3e), play that turns rough from arousal and size in place of a die, and listening stops at the waypoints and after a heard chorus in place of a schedule (E4m), the hunt weighed by the energy the hunter will need rather than by how full his gut is (E4n), walking at the speed the body sets in place of a copied speed (E2i), moving within a crown without climbing down to the ground first (E1q), an unanswered departure that ends in the leaver’s own decision in place of two timers (E5f), a guardian who protects while its ward cannot hold its own in place of an age limit (E4o), no fixed gap after an animal’s own aggression or display (E4q), and, new in S39, no fixed gap after a call, with an alarm repeated while the caller sees a community member that has not learnt of the snake (E5g), and trips that remember a crown found empty and walk to a caller’s crown (E3h). Re-deciding (E3d) passed with E5f and E4o as S32 but cost every class reserve, so the stack fell back to S31 by its registered condition; S34, S37 and S39 build on it.

Most stage results quoted on this page were scored on the protocol in force before 5 October. That day a new freeze applied the ten audited target and scorer patches (the old bands are kept for comparison). Bands changed: T-HUN-1 5–25 became 4–11, which puts S39’s 16.9 outside, and T-RNG-5 0.3–0.6 became 0.3–0.75, among others. S39 and today’s model were then re-baselined on the new protocol at 60 days, 6 months and 12 months (e-rebaseline.md): the figures are in Feeding and foraging. The Recurrent Decision Model (Track R) is planned, not a result: in every benchmark so far the rules made 100% of the choices (The choice itself).

Stages not yet integrated, held off, recorded or still in progress are drawn as separate layers, dashed, and never counted in S39.

How to read this page

Each diagram follows one kind of decision from the body state that drives it to the act. Read the middle column top to bottom; boxes at the sides feed the step they point at. Every box carries one status, shown by its colour, its icon and its label.

  • Prescribed

    Still prescribed in S39. A clock hour, a timer, a dice roll, a quota, a tuned bonus or a field value of the behaviour itself states when or how often something happens. Named by its parameter or its line of code.

  • Replaced

    Prescribed on today’s model, replaced in S39. Its detail shows Before (the old rule, its parameters and values), Now (the mechanism), the switch that did it, the stage and its verdict.

  • Input

    Physiology or physics measured apart from the behaviour, with its evidence tag: H high, M medium, L low, or assumed.

  • Design

    A design weight or structure that states no outcome. Not counted as a prescription.

  • Not in S39

    A separate layer, dashed: a candidate not yet integrated, a defect fix, or a stage held off, recorded or in progress. A dashed pink arrow points at what it would change.

Hover, focus or tap

Point at a box, tab to it, or tap it for its detail. Click or tap pins the detail open; tap elsewhere or press Esc to close it. Every diagram also has a text version below it.

Expand

Opens a diagram full screen. Zoom with the scroll wheel, a pinch, or + and −; pan by dragging or with the arrow keys; 0 fits it to the screen. Esc, the close button or a click outside closes it.

What counts as a prescription

The prescription ledger’s rule: entries of the parameter registry classed outcome-encoding and in use under the field profile, plus lines of src/sim that compare the hour of the day, roll dice against a fixed number, filter acts by the time of day, hold an act back for a fixed time after the animal’s own last one, or give a fixed bonus for carrying on with an act (the last two counted since E0b). Score weights written into the code are design and are not counted.

Where the numbers come from

Every list, value, count and switch on this page is generated from scripts/prescription-ledger.ts; the words come from the stage pre-registrations and the code. See how this page is made.

01

The choice itself

In every benchmark so far the rules made 100% of the choices; no decision model has contributed to any scorecard number. The same machinery picks every act. Since S21 no fitted temperature sets how sharp a choice is (E3e). Three fixed numbers still set how sticky choices are; E3d, which would replace two, passed but was taken off the stack for its energy cost.

3 still prescribed1 replaced in S39of 4 on today's model

  • Where behaviour comes from today: the loop is code by design, the rules make the choice, and 42 prescriptions are left inside the mechanisms on S39, against 147 on today's model. Of the 150 targets in data/targets.json (38 fitted, 112 held-out), S39 has about 30 rows inside their field band and 31 outside at 6 months, and 36 and 37 at 12 months (means of 4 runs); today's model has 27 and 32 at 6 months. The other rows are not scored as inside or outside (at 6 months: inconclusive, insufficient, not applicable, not scorable or sealed) (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md, Track R, direction amendment of 6 October; e-rebaseline.md).
  • The Recurrent Decision Model is a memoryless decision kernel (the rules, GLiNER2.5-Decide, a stand-in or Jev) called at event-driven decision points inside a loop that carries internal state on several timescales and local perception. It is planned, not a result. Its three claims are open: (1) mechanistic state beats prescribed behaviour on the same kernel (partly shown by Track E); (2) any kernel works in the loop (not shown); (3) a small kernel in a good loop matches an expensive one (not shown) (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md, Track R).
  • The user's direction of 6 October has three aims: less of the behaviour should come from hand-written code (A), the yardstick is what wild chimpanzees do (B), and the choice should be made by a simple decision engine, GLiNER2.5-Decide as the base, inside the loop (C). The user's decisions of the same day: wadging 0.5 is the working base for engine comparisons (see Feeding and foraging); the rules' pick stays on the menu for the main comparison and "removed" is reported as a second number; the random-engine test is judged on daytime rows and drives animals aged 8 and over; the wild-choice benchmark goes ahead as a test, with field labels in a separate, small, named adapter; and five targets (T-SOC-1, T-SOC-2, T-FIS-1, T-FIS-3, T-FIS-5) are marked compromised for any engine trained on the benchmark's training part (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md, Track R).
  • M1–M3 (done): GLiNER as it is cannot choose for the chimpanzees on Track E's state. Untuned, it agrees with the rules on 28% of 3,067 decision points (chance 19%), and it moves with the drive words, not with reserves, sleep pressure, water or heat. In the loop, five days, ten focal animals on seeds 48 and 7: 1,028 kcal a day against the rules' 1,603, a loss of 1.05% of the store a day, 80% of the night out of a nest (rules 2.9%); feeding was inside its band for 0 of 10 animals (rules 5). Wording that follows Track E's mechanics puts them back in their nests (2.8% of the night out) but not back to feeding (945 kcal a day). Asked at every decision point, even the rules' own best option walks 1.7 times as far (3.77 against 2.17 km a day); with the rules' intention gate it matches the rules. Jev, run on 300 draws, reads the body's numbers and agrees on 35%; its outputs cannot be used as labels (em-prereg.md; agent-reported, spot-checked by the integrator).
  • R1 (built and merged, tests only): one kernel interface and five kernels, with three switches that are 0 by default, so the world and the request are today's. The intention gate lives in the loop and every kernel other than the rules shares it. "Withheld" means the rules' pick is removed from the menu. Inside the tick the rules still read the live animal, a stated exception to parity that stage R2 is to close. (r1-prereg.md; IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md, Stage R1).
  • RW (done as a test, development part only): choosing whom a wild adult male grooms, 448 choices from 30 males at Ngogo. Random 0.18; nearest at the last scan 0.315; most frequent past partner 0.43; the three-rule stack 0.49; Codex (an outside model, as run by the tool, effort low, batches of 8) 0.49, no difference from the stack (paired +0.007); untuned GLiNER 0.37, which is 0.52 against the stack's 0.54 on menus of 8 or fewer (no difference) and 0.18 against 0.42 on wider ones, where it falls to chance. So a small engine can match three simple rules on small menus; the packet, not the reader, limits the score (rw-bench-prereg.md §13 A5 and A7; rw-prereg.md).
  • Planned, not results: R1b (the same loop with the rules kernel and the null kernel, 60 days, 5 seeds, on S39 at a swallowed share of 0.5 with 0.25 as the sensitivity arm, to find which rows the choice moves); R2 (state in the packet, the night and dusk menus aligned across kernels, and three techniques from the Jev documentation applied to the small kernel: fan-out for wide menus, confidence-gated routing and "kind of activity first, then target", each behind a switch that is 0, measured on RW first); R3 to R6 are behind the start gate and not started (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md, Track R). The three-year runs are registered (e-years-prereg.md) and have no result.
  • The rules policy is the same for every domain below; a model can replace the pick, never the checks (simulation.md §8).
  • E3 is not part of S39. Its stage stopped after three iterations (e3-prereg.md).
  • E3e found the temperature set 22% of resting, 30% of grooming and 18% of feeding time by picks the animal's knowledge did not explain. On S21 (S19 with E3e's switch) departures before sunrise fall from 0.81 to 0.58, the fruit share rises to 0.759, and animals walk less (males 2.19 km a day against 2.77) (e3e-prereg.md, e-stack2-confirm.md).
  • Re-deciding (E3d) passed alone on S27 (as S28) and with E5f and E4o (as S32), but cost every class reserve. E3g found the cost in the trip valuation itself: trips deliver 13–31% of the meal they are valued at, and two thirds never feed at their target. By the registered condition the stack fell back to S31, and re-deciding stays off until trips stop failing (e3g-prereg.md, e-stack2-confirm.md).

02

Feeding and foraging

S39 starves at 12 months: 6 deaths in 20 seed-runs, in three of its four runs, none in the 6-month runs; at 60 days and at 6 months none starve. Hunger comes from an energy ledger and a gut, and since S13 where to eat comes from the net energy rate each option promises. Since S27 the body sets the walking speed (E2i) and an animal moves within the crown it is in without climbing down (E1q). Still fitted: how fast a crown empties.

1 still prescribed9 replaced in S39of 10 on today's model

  • S39 is not viable at 12 months. Starvation deaths: 6 in 20 seed-runs (four runs of five seeds), two each in three runs and none in the fourth: three adolescents, two pregnant females and one infant of 2–5 years. None came in the 6-month runs. Today's model has none, because it has no energy ledger and cannot starve this way. At 60 days S39 has no starvation death; at 6 months neither (one of its four runs fails births against deaths: 10 births, 12 deaths). The reserves show a lean season: between days 45 and 230 of the scored year (mid-December to mid-June in the world's calendar) juveniles fall to about −0.20 of their store and nursing mothers to −0.13, then partly recover. Every Track E keep decision so far was made on 60-day windows starting in late October, before this season, so none could see it (e-rebaseline.md, Part C).
  • On the new bands S39 is nearer the field than today's model at every horizon, and that is not progress on the rows both protocols score. Summed band distance, lower is better, means of 4 runs, new bands of freeze 5d4fa5a2a500bce6. Held-out rows: 6.65 against 11.97 at 60 days, 8.82 against 13.74 at 6 months, 15.10 against 21.83 at 12 months (S39's z against today's model −4.2, −2.9, −4.1). Fitted rows: 1.52 against 3.68, 2.69 against 3.65, 4.17 against 8.94. The row set grows with the horizon, so the sums do not compare across horizons. At 6 months, the 13 held-out rows whose band did not change are about even (−0.31; +0.80 without the rare rows); the 10 new rows give −5.40, nearly all from two infant rows today's model fails (T-INF-6, T-INF-2). On the old bands S39 is worse at 6 months (held-out 11.11 against 7.61, z +2.2). By the registered 6-month rule S39 held and its group was extended to 12 months (e-rebaseline.md).
  • Walk-back, seed 48 only (stopped once every arm had starved): S37, S34, S31 and S27 starve, with 3, 3, 4 and 2 deaths, where S39's four runs have 0, 0, 1 and 0. By the registered rule the cause is older than S27: the energy ledger's response to the lean season. The later switches seem to reduce starvation, not cause it, on this one seed (e-rebaseline.md).
  • E1r, diagnosis (nothing changed): the energy absorbed through the gut is the main term; expenditure is second; what makes the deficit lethal is a valuation trap (confidence moderate to high). In the lean season the diet turns to figs and fallback foods, which give 22% and 33% less absorbed energy per gram of dry matter than drupes (1.83 and 1.57 against 2.36 kcal/g). The foregut is full in 76–89% of the eating minutes of small and reproducing females in March and April, so their absorbed energy falls 50–120 kcal a day while their feeding time rises up to twofold. Below about −0.3 of the store, E1i's satiation and forageRate keep a depleted animal on the fallback food the gut passes least energy from (e1r-prereg.md §9 to §11).
  • E1s, stopped after 3 iterations, none passing; gutValue stays 0. Valuing options by the bout the gut allows removes the trap (depleted animals eat drupes, the fallback 12% of their decisions against 31–47%), but makes moving almost free: depleted animals walk 6.8–8.8 km a day against 2.9–3.6 and starve sooner. Starvation deaths on seeds 48 and 7 in 6 months: 11, 9 and 3 in iterations 1 to 3, and 0 for S39. This runs against the field directions (shorter journeys and more fallback when food is scarce) (e1s-prereg.md §8.8).
  • E1t, not kept; horizonLived stays 0. It fixes a bug (the feeding horizon was right within an hour in 4.2% of daylight decisions on S39, 99.9% with the fix). At 6 months it passed with no starvation. At 12 months it had 8 starvation deaths in 20 seed-runs against S39's 6, and the rule kept it only at 6 or fewer; if it changed nothing, 8 or more would occur with probability 0.26 (Poisson), so the result shows neither harm nor benefit. The bug stays in S39 (e1t-prereg.md §8, §9).
  • E1u, audit of the gut's inputs (research only, nothing applied): most gut inputs have no chimpanzee data. No study has weighed a wadge, so the swallowed share of pith is unknown. The sourced range of fibre digestibility widened from 0.449–0.543 to 0.23–0.543; it now ranks first by how far it moves the deficit (448 kcal a day), ahead of whole-gut volume (423) and the hindgut's dry matter (392). A fermentation yield of 2.6 kcal/g in place of 3.0 is proposed, not applied (e1u-prereg.md §5 and §6).
  • E1v, wadging as a tested range: starvation deaths at 12 months were 6 in 20 seed-runs with all the pith's fibre swallowed (S39 as it is), 0 in 20 at a swallowed share of 0.5 and 0 in 20 at 0.25; at 6 months all three have 0. So S39's viability at 12 months depends on an input nobody has measured. No value is adopted as a claim about chimpanzees; 0.5 is the declared working base for engine comparisons and 0.25 the sensitivity check, to be revisited after the three-year results; the default stays 1 (user, 6 October 2026). 0 in 20 does not show the rate is zero: it fits a true rate of up to about 3 in 20 (95% bound). The non-fruit share of eating time fell or held in every class (pregnant females 0.36 → 0.28 and 0.27), not rose.
  • Open, from E1v: at 0.5, two of four runs have a fitted sum past the noise rule, driven by one row, first-year mortality (T-DEM-1: 0.32 and 0.40 against a band of 0.11–0.19), from illness in infants under six months (4 and 6 deaths, none starvation). Whether the energy ledger changes an infant's illness risk was not checked (e1v-prereg.md §8.4, §8.5).
  • Planned, not a result: three years in one run (S39 at a swallowed share of 0.25 and 0.5, two runs each, and today's model, 5 seeds, 1,095 scored days) to see whether animals starve in a second and third lean season and whether the population is sustained (e-years-prereg.md).
  • On S39 the feeding row is 0.368 and rest is 0.443 (band 0.30–0.47), adult males walk 2.77 km a day (S27's runs 3.34), animals feed in 9.4 trees a day, as on S27, and the fruit share is above its band (0.826; band 0.6–0.78). One animal died on S39 in 5 seeds × 60 days, an adolescent of illness; none starved (60 days, before the 5 October freeze; the 12-month result is above) (e-stack2-confirm.md).
  • Valuing food by rate exposed two prescriptions the weights had compensated: the choice's noise (rgTemperature) blurred rate differences, and a crop beyond one bout adds nothing, so choices do not follow the crop (e3c-prereg.md §8). Since S21 the temperature is gone and a tree out of sight is valued at a crop drawn from the animal's belief (see the choice itself); a crop beyond one bout still adds nothing (e3e-prereg.md §8).
  • On S27 the longer walks paid for themselves in food: adult males took in 48 kcal a day more and spent 47 more, nursing mothers +45 and +33, juveniles +23 and +25 (against S25's runs), as the walks reached more fruit. On S39 animals walk less (adult males 2.77 km a day) as more trips find food; re-deciding, which added more than a kilometre a day, is off the stack (see the choice itself) (e-stack2-confirm.md).
  • Open: the field figure of about ten times more energy walking than climbing was priced with a human rock-climbing equation; at the ledger's cost per metre, which measured climbing supports, the field's own distances read 1.9–2.9, so a climbed metre is not too dear. The model climbs about twice the field's implied metres; infants still climb from crowns' edges, and the heat balance and the ledger price a climbed metre differently (e1q-prereg.md §7, §8).
  • Open: nursing mothers stop eating with room in the gut, so the limit is their appetite and their day, not the gut wall (e1h-prereg.md §9, e1i-prereg.md).

Thirst timers are gone. Thirst is read from a water balance in millilitres, so animals drink when they run short, not on a schedule.

The nest clock, the morning bout and the night menu are gone. Sleep pressure, a circadian sleep gate, light and company now decide when a chimp nests and when it leaves.

The midday rest clock is gone. Rest and shelter follow a heat balance, which at Kibale's temperatures stores almost no heat.

06

Nursing and weaning

Mothers pay for the milk their infants drink and decide, by their own deficit, when to let them suckle: the weaning roll is gone. Milk now falls with the infant's age and growth yields to the store, but weaning itself does not emerge; its age is still drawn.

0 still prescribed3 replaced in S39of 3 on today's model

  • On S39 nursing mothers lose 0.046% of their store a day (S27's four runs 0.079) and their balance sits between −12 and −35 kcal a day by infant age. Infants of 1–2, 2–3 and 3–4 years drink 282, 270 and 259 kcal a day (cap 307) (e-stack2-confirm.md, S39).
  • Infants' reserves fall 0.082% and 0.022% of the store a day at 0.5–2 and 2–5 years (S27's four runs 0.102 and 0.064), and they grow 3.47–3.50 kg a year at 1–4 years, still about twice Gombe's 1.6 (e-stack2-confirm.md, e1p-prereg.md §3).
  • Open: nothing drives milk to zero before the drawn weaning age (e1o-prereg.md §5).

07

Grooming and play

Since S9 the social need is relationship upkeep: it rises by what the animal's bonds lose each day, only grooming meets it, and grooming's partner terms count only as far as the groomer needs grooming. Since S22 play turns rough only when a player is acutely aroused relative to the size gap, not by a die (E4m). Still prescribed: the fixed terms that carry a grooming bout on.

What is still prescribed

On S39, 42 prescriptions remain. These are the biggest, by how much of a chimp’s life they reach. None of them is the choice maker: the rules make every choice in every benchmark so far, and a decision model has not yet replaced them.

  • The choice itself (3)

    How long an intention lasts, the bonus for carrying on and the penalty for repeating an act just finished touch every decision; since S21 no temperature sets how sharp a choice is (E3e). E3d, which would replace the first two, passed but was taken off the stack for its energy cost; urgency (E3) stopped after three iterations.

  • Foraging (1)

    The fruit eaten per hour, fitted to the day range and travel share, enters the energy rate of every feeding option since S13. Since S27 the body sets the walking speed (E2i).

  • Killing (12)

    Gang attacks and infanticide are impulses and kill probabilities. Patrolling has no counted prescription left: the males decide (E4i), at a real fusion (E4j), and since S22 they stop to listen at the waypoints and after a heard chorus (E4m).

  • Fights (1) and hunts (1)

    Serious wounds, and meat eaten per hour. Contact, support and the repetition of charges follow each animal’s state and assessment (E4h, E5e), with no fixed gap after its own last one since S34 (E4q); a hunt is valued as food (E4e), weighed by the energy need (E4n), and succeeds by the pursuit itself (E4k).

  • Life, death and care (12), mating and dispersal (6)

    Epidemics, snares, adoption, carrying the dead and bereavement; mating quotas, a transfer hazard and a disperser share. Since S31 a guardian protects its ward while the ward cannot hold its own, not to an age (E4o).

  • Calls (5)

    How calls sound (drumming and voice signatures). Since S37 no fixed gap follows a call, and an alarm at a snake is repeated while the caller sees an animal that has not learnt of it (E5g). Staying together has none left: since S31 an unanswered departure ends in the leaver’s own decision (E5f).

  • Grooming (1)

    The fixed terms that carry a grooming bout on, counted since E0b; E3d, off the stack, would drop them with the bonus for carrying on.

  • Who chooses (not counted)

    The rules pick every act in every benchmark so far; no decision model has contributed to a scorecard number. Untuned GLiNER agrees with the rules on 28% of decision points on S39 (chance 19%) and underfeeds, so it cannot take the choice as it is (em-prereg.md). The Recurrent Decision Model that would put an engine in the loop is planned (The choice itself).

By the ledger’s kinds, the 42 are: 12 probability, 6 field-copy, 6 fitted, 3 designed-from-target, 3 hazard, 3 quota, 3 quota (code), 2 bonus, 1 bonus (code), 1 clock, 1 dice (code), 1 timer.

Open problems

The first six are the open questions of Track R and of E1v; the rest are what S39 still gets wrong, from the integrated confirms and the stage records, mostly scored before the new freeze of 5 October. S39 has since been re-baselined on it, and the rows below say where that changes a figure.

  • S39 starves at 12 months unless some pith fibre is spat out

    With all the pith’s fibre swallowed there are six starvation deaths in 20 seed-runs over 12 months and none in the 6-month runs; at a swallowed share of 0.5 or 0.25 there are none in 20. Zero in 20 is compatible with a true rate of up to about 3 in 20. Every Track E keep decision was made on 60-day windows, before the lean season (e1v §8.4, e-rebaseline.md).

  • Wadging has no measured value

    No wadge has been weighed, so the swallowed share is unknown. The user adopted no value as a claim about chimpanzees: 0.5 is the declared working base for engine comparisons and 0.25 the sensitivity check, to be revisited after the three-year results. Those runs are registered and planned; they have no result (e1v §8.5, e-years-prereg.md).

  • Infant illness at a swallowed share of 0.5

    At 0.5, two of four runs have a fitted sum past the noise rule, driven by first-year mortality (T-DEM-1 0.32 and 0.40 against a band of 0.11–0.19), from illness in infants under six months. Whether the energy ledger changes an infant’s illness risk was not checked (e1v §8.4).

  • The choice is still made by the rules

    Planned, not done: R1b (the same loop with the rules kernel and the null kernel, to find which rows the choice moves) and R2 (state in the packet, aligned night menus, fan-out for wide menus, confidence-gated routing); R3 to R6 are behind the start gate. The Recurrent Decision Model’s three claims are open: state beats prescription on one kernel (partly shown), any kernel works in the loop (not shown), a small kernel matches an expensive one (not shown). Untuned GLiNER agrees with the rules on 28% of decision points (em-prereg.md; IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md, Track R).

  • The wild yardstick covers one choice

    The wild-choice benchmark scores whom an adult male grooms at Ngogo: random 0.18, the three-rule stack 0.49, Codex 0.49, untuned GLiNER 0.37 (0.52 on menus of 8 or fewer, 0.18 on wider ones). The records hold no rank, kinship, age, body state or food, so nothing is yet known about feeding choices. The sealed held-out part is not opened (rw-bench-prereg.md §13).

  • Behaviour still comes from code

    S39 has 42 prescriptions against 147 on today’s model. At 12 months it has about 36 target rows inside their field band and 37 outside (means of 4 runs; today’s model at 6 months: 27 and 32, S39 30 and 31). Aim: less behaviour from hand-written code, judged against wild chimpanzees (IMPLEMENTATION_PLAN.md, Track R, direction amendment).

  • Re-deciding still costs a kilometre

    Re-deciding (E3d) stays off the stack: even with E3h’s corrected beliefs it adds about a kilometre a day to every class, and the same climbing. The keep test turns the valuation’s preference for trips over staying into a trip at every bout end; the corrections remove only its futile part (e3h §8, e3g §8).

  • Trips that fail at emptied crowns

    About 40% of trips still fail, mostly at crowns emptied since the sighting by animals the traveller never saw there, and by the end of ripening. A belief that expects others’ visits would need each animal’s own estimate of how often crowns are visited, a learned rate, which E3g showed collapses travel when applied to every trip (e3h §8).

  • The mating quota

    A male mates at most once per 1.5 hours and waits 0.5 h after a failed approach, a swollen female solicits at most once per 0.3 h after mating, and a guarding male chases a rival at most once per 0.25 h. E4p replaced all four with the partner’s consent and the paternity a copulation adds: every version passed the keep rule in quick mode, but none met the registered rate line, so the quota stays and E4p is a recorded layer. Its premise that few males attend a swollen female was a misreading: the 4–12 males it cited are the community’s sex ratio, which the model matches (e4p §10.3).

  • Departures before sunrise

    On S39 the observer scores 0.613 of departures before sunrise (S27’s four runs 0.660), against the old one-site band of 0.08–0.30 (T-FOOD-10). E2h found the row scored differently from the field: the new freeze applies its scorer fix and a two-site band of 0.08–0.78; at 6 months S39 scores 0.905 on it, still outside, by 0.18 on average (e-stack2-confirm.md, e2h).

  • Alarm calls and their audience

    Alarm calls that track what the audience knows score 0.023 on S39, far below their band of 0.25–0.55 (T-COM-11; S27’s runs 0.071–0.175). No gap sets it: everyone within 8 m sees the snake model at once and one hoo informs everyone within 100 m, so the audience almost always knows, where the field’s trials met the model one animal at a time on a travel path. A question for the snake-trial protocol, not for the stack’s switches (e5g §7.2, e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • Rare events: not testable at 12 months

    The 90-day cap is lifted: runs of up to two years are allowed, worked up from 6 to 12 to 24 months only when a test needs it. By the ledger’s grouping 27 of S39’s 42 prescriptions are rare-event rules (lethal conflict and injury 13; deaths, adoption and bereavement 8; disease and snares 4; dispersal 2). At 12 months no rare-event family has enough events on its own outcome rows (3 killings against a minimum of 10), so none is staged there. They go to 24 months on a viable base, which is planned, not run (e-rebaseline.md, Parts D1 and D2).

  • Travel just under its band

    With trips that find food animals walk less (adult males 2.77 km a day against 3.34 in S27’s runs), and the travel share falls just under its band (T-ACT-2 0.115, band 0.12–0.25) (e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • Male aggression’s structure

    Since S34 no fixed gap holds back a charge or a display; E4q found the gaps only trimmed rates the acts’ own scores set. What is wrong is the structure: mate-guard charges are 59% of adult males’ charges, so male–male aggression per dyad-hour is 6.5 times Kanyawara’s, while chases and attacks are 10–20 times fewer and contact 5 times less (e4q §7).

  • Hunting beyond energy

    On S39 the model hunts 16.9 times per community-year, inside its band of 5–25, but hunts succeed less often than in the field (0.396; band 0.5–0.8) and every success takes one monkey. E3g’s correction, a colobus group seen within the hour not met anew, passed twice but cost other females a little reserve both times, so it is not adopted. The hunt is valued as energy alone: why chimpanzees hunt beyond energy has a direction and no magnitude in any source read (e3g, e4n §2.2, e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • Patrols: fewer and deeper

    On S39 patrols run 0.062 a week, below their band of 0.1–0.5 (T-PAT-1), and 0.89 of them enter the neighbours’ range, above its band of 0.4–0.7 (T-PAT-6), while intergroup encounters sit inside theirs (7.3 per community-year). Listening has no value in the model’s perception, so a stop gains only time to count a chorus, and how often patrols happen still follows design constants set while choices were noisy (e4m §11, e4j, e3e §6.3).

  • Climbing: metres, not cost

    The field figure of about ten times more energy walking than climbing was priced with a human rock-climbing equation; at the ledger’s cost per metre, which measured climbing supports, the field’s own distances read 1.9–2.9. The model climbs about twice the field’s implied metres, a third of them back into the crown just left; moving within the crown cuts climbing 7–25% by class (S26), and on S39 climbing is a little below S27’s. Infants still climb from crowns’ edges, and the heat balance and the ledger price a climbed metre differently (e1q §7, §8, e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • Meat’s hourly rate

    Meat is eaten at 0.35 of a carcass an hour, so one capture is worth 1,149 kcal and 2.9 hours of eating, and the same value enters the hunt’s worth. The edible energy of a red colobus capture could not be sourced, nor the mass of the immature monkeys that make most kills (e4m §11, e4o).

  • The jitter’s size was never derived

    Since S21 a choice varies only through the rules’ ±0.12 jitter, kept as the animal’s error in valuing the options it knows, and its uncertainty about trees out of sight. The jitter’s span (0.24, design) was never derived or sourced, and it is a hash, not a draw. A sourced estimation error, such as a Weber fraction for food quantity, or the value terms still missing would replace it (e3e §7, §8).

  • Switching costs and a crop’s worth beyond one bout

    With no fitted noise to hide them, value terms the model lacks show: there is no cost of switching an act in place, a crop beyond one bout adds nothing to a trip, and meals have no dynamics of their own. Without the jitter (E3e’s first iteration) acts were dropped at every re-decision and animals went to the nearest crown (e3e §8, e3c).

  • No violence on patrols

    Patrols rarely meet neighbours as the observer scores it (T-PAT-7 0 on S39 and 0–0.06 in S27’s four runs, band 0.15–0.45), and in the S15 runs none turned violent: the observer’s contact needs a seen encounter, while patrols mostly hear their neighbours (e4i, e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • Reserves still fall, more slowly

    On S39 every class’s reserve trend is better than in S27’s runs: nursing mothers lose 0.046% of their store a day, adult males 0.014%, juveniles 0.043% and infants of 0.5–2 years 0.082%. Every class but the youngest infants still loses reserves, and infants grow at about twice Gombe’s rate (e-stack2-confirm.md).

  • Wounds without a field anchor

    Since S13 a strike’s wound scales with the striker’s mass over the struck animal’s (design); in E4h wounds of 0.05 or more came out seven times the reference’s, and no field measure of damage per contact exists to judge it. Three contest rows are in the new freeze of 5 October; at 6 months S39 scores contact share T-SOC-14 0.069 (band 0.08–0.37, outside), contact by rank difference T-SOC-15 inside, and coalitionary share T-SOC-16 0.24 (band 0.03–0.2, inconclusive) (e-rebaseline.md; e4h).

  • Company valued by a need only grooming relieves

    E5a values a companion’s company by the social need, but company does not relieve it; only grooming does, so time that grooming no longer fills goes to seeking company rather than rest. The bond dynamics the need follows are design (e5d).

  • Weaning does not emerge

    On S39 infants of 1–4 years drink 259–282 kcal of milk a day (cap 307), and nothing drives it to zero. Weaning age is still drawn, between 4.1 and 5.2 years; the ledger classes it as an input, although it builds the weaning-age target in (e1o, e1n).

  • Feeding together

    Party members feed one or two at a time (1.3 per crown) and a visit eats about 11% of a crown, so the crop never limits them and party size does not follow it (S39: 4.05 animals, band 3–9); valued by its energy rate, a crop beyond one bout adds nothing. What makes them arrive and feed together is not in the model (e5c, e3c).

  • Rough play from play itself

    Since S22 play turns rough only when a player is acutely aroused, from storms or aggression received, so it almost never does: the model’s play has no exchange of advantage that could build arousal, while field play fighting is a contest (e4m §11).

  • Patterns that do not emerge yet

    A midday rest (e2a) and a daily course of calls (e4c, e4d).

  • The gut’s size

    The only chimpanzee gut volume is one captive animal of unknown mass: 57–76 mL per kg at captive female masses, against the 83 assumed (handoff §4.1).

  • Everything is provisional

    Every keep is provisional until a 365-day run, and S39 itself failed its 12-month run without wadging. No switch goes on by default before the C11 calibration and the combined proof are frozen (handoff §1, §5.7).
