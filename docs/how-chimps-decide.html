How a chimp decides

Code writes the menu. The chimp's own state sets the scores. A weighted draw, or a model, picks one. No storyline is scripted.

At 1 min/s the clock runs 4 ticks per real second. At 1 day/s it runs 5,760. Chimps update one after another, so each sees what earlier ones just did.

How long things stay in mind

Where others are is forgotten in minutes. Fruit trees and grudges last weeks. A summary of the social year lasts for life.

Places

  • Up to 60 fruit trees, each with the crop last seen there
  • Up to 48 other things: animals, colobus, water
  • Remembered trees are why it travels out of sight

Recent events

  • 12 short first-person episodes, like "lost a fight with…"
  • Shown to the model as "5 min ago: …"

Relationships

  • A running tally per partner: grooming hours, support, threats, attacks, make-ups, matings, meat
  • Folded into monthly, then yearly digests

Night closes the menu

After dark only nest, rest, nurse, flee, alarm, shelter and submit stay open, plus self-defence. Dusk keeps a last feed, drink or groom but drops travel, hunts, patrols and play.

Momentum is built in

The act already under way gets +0.25. An act that just finished gets −0.5. So chimps tend to carry on, and do not repeat what they just completed.

How the rules pick

First a check: has anything changed? If not, carry on. If so, draw from the short menu, weighted by score.

Has anything changed since the last choice?

  • It was interrupted
  • A need crossed a level (mild 0.4, moderate 0.55, strong 0.7, severe 0.88)
  • The part of the day changed
  • 30 minutes have passed
  • The act ended or is no longer legal
  • While feeding: a known place pays at least 2× better

When a model picks instead

The model replaces step 4 only. It gets the same short menu, sees only what that chimp perceives, and its answer is checked twice before it counts.

SimulationDecision loopModel
Simulation1The chimp reaches a decision moment and waits. It keeps its current act if still legal, otherwise rests.
Decision loop2Takes one waiting chimp. The one you selected goes first, then the longest wait. One request at a time.
Decision loop3Builds the packet: that chimp's own percept and up to 8 options, in a fixed order that hides the rules' ranking.
Model4Returns one probability per option. About 0.25 to 0.4 s on the local server.
Decision loop5Checks the answer: a real option, probabilities that match the menu.
Simulation6Re-checks the option is still legal now, then starts the act. Stale or illegal answers are discarded.

Three modes

  • Off. Rules decide for everyone.
  • Async (default). The world keeps running. After 6 forest-minutes or 6 real seconds of waiting, rules decide.
  • Lockstep. The clock stops until the answer comes, up to 6 real seconds.

Who the model drives

  • Selected (default): the one chimp you clicked.
  • Focal set: up to 6. Selected, each alpha, a mother, a juvenile.
  • All.

Rules step in when

  • The model is not loaded or errors
  • The answer is invalid
  • No answer in time
  • Fewer than 2 options

What it is never told
  • Anything the chimp cannot see, hear or remember
  • Ids or world state
  • Which option the rules prefer
  • Instructions from the browser. The prompt is built from checked fields only.

Three interchangeable choosers: GLiNER in the browser (default), GLiNER on the local server, or the Jev API. Today the model agrees with the rules' top pick about 40 to 65% of the time and leans social.

Fixed, chance, or emergent

The rules of the game are hardcoded. What happens in the game is not.

fixed in code
  • The 15 s tick and its order
  • The 30 possible acts
  • Who may do what: age, sex and situation gates
  • Every score formula and weight
  • Night and dusk menus
  • Bout length ranges
  • How fast needs rise
  • Sense ranges, memory lifetimes
  • How fights, hunts and matings resolve
seeded dice
  • The pick from the short menu
  • Rare urges that add an option: patrol, gang attack, fight escalation, infanticide, rain display
  • Who wins a contest, who is wounded
  • Weather changes
  • Conception, death, a female leaving home

Same seed, same inputs: the same world, every time.

emerges from state
  • Parties splitting and merging
  • Community ranges: the area actually used, redrawn daily
  • Rank order and alpha changes
  • Alliances: the top 3 partners by bond and support
  • Grudges and avoidance
  • Where a patrol heads: the border left unvisited longest
  • Meetings between communities
from outside
  • The model's pick, when it is on
  • Field experiments you trigger: a storm, a snake, played-back calls, removing the alpha
  • Life speed: natural, or one year per day

A live model's timing cannot be replayed from the seed alone.
